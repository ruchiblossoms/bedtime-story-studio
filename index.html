Under the hood · the whole product fits on this slide

No app. Twenty lines of instructions.

You are the Bedtime Story Studio — a warm, imaginative storyteller for young children ages 3–8.

WHEN THE USER OPENS A NEW CHAT, greet them and ask for exactly three things:
  1. Who's the story for? (child's name)
  2. Three story ingredients (character, place, or thing)
  3. How long? — short / medium / long

WHEN YOU HAVE THOSE THREE THINGS, write the story immediately — no acknowledgment, no preamble, just start.

STORY RULES:
  · Weave in ALL three ingredients naturally
  · Use the child's name several times
  · Vivid but soothing — no scary or high-stakes tension
  · Small adventure → moment of wonder → peaceful ending
  · Word counts: short 100–200 · medium 200–400 · long 400–600

Under the hood · boringly simple

Three layers. Zero code.

01 · Prompt
A system prompt pinned to a Claude Project. Defines the voice, the three-question intake, the story rules. Every new chat in the project starts here.
↓
02 · Model
Claude — one of the frontier models, chosen once in the project settings. Handles the creativity, the name-weaving, the soothing tone. No fine-tune, no RAG, no backend.
↓
03 · Surface
claude.ai chat. That's the whole UI. Runs on phone, tablet, laptop. Voice mode = story read aloud. Zero install, zero deploy, works everywhere the browser does.

Total build time: about 15 minutes. Total maintenance: change one line if I want a new rule.

09 / 12
Learnings — from the PM chair

The prompt IS the product.

When there's no code, every product decision lives in the words. That changes how you iterate.

01 Constraints are features
"No preamble, no titles, no meta" isn't a rule — it's the reason bedtime doesn't stall. The things you forbid shape the experience more than the things you invite.
02 Ship it, then live with it
Every rule in the prompt came from an actual bedtime where the story fell flat. "Use the name several times" was added the night my kid said "but is it about ME?"
03 Voice > features
Kids don't care about your UI. They care whether the story sounds like a bedtime story. Half the prompt is tone-setting. That's not decoration — that's the product.
04 Iterate in one line
A "release" is: edit the prompt, save, next chat gets the new version. No PR, no review, no deploy. The feedback loop is a bedtime.
10 / 12
The bigger shift

I used to think products needed an app.

Before

To have a "product" it needed a domain, a UI, a database, a deploy. A bedtime story generator meant at least a weekend of code. So I never built one.

After Aha

A product is a repeatable experience someone loves. If a 20-line prompt inside Claude delivers that experience — that's the product. The infrastructure is a detail.

The recipe

Pick a real moment in your life →  name what would make it better →  write the rules as if you were briefing a smart intern →  save it as a Claude Project — you now have a product.

The bar was lower than I thought. If you can describe it, you can ship it.
